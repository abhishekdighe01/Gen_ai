## Detailed Question Explanations

### Q1: How much subsidy will I get for a 3 kW rooftop solar system?

**Expected Answer:**  
For a 3 kW rooftop solar system in general category states, you will receive **₹78,000** Central Financial Assistance (CFA).

**Calculation:**
- First 2 kW: 2.0 × ₹30,000 = ₹60,000
- Additional 1 kW: 1.0 × ₹18,000 = ₹18,000
- **Total CFA: ₹78,000**

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(h)

---

### Q2: How much terrace area do I need for 1 kW?

**Expected Answer:**  
Approximately **10 square meters (100 sq ft)** per kW of solar panel capacity. This can vary from 9-12 sq m depending on panel efficiency and installation layout.

**Context:** Standard 1 kW solar systems use 3-4 panels of 330-400W each. Each panel is roughly 2m × 1m = 2 sq m. With spacing for mounting and shadows, 10 sq m per kW is a safe estimate.

For the Kopargaon homeowner with 40 sq m terrace: Can fit approximately 4 kW capacity, but CFA is capped at 3 kW.

---

### Q3: What is the minimum warranty on solar panels under this scheme?

**Expected Answer:**  
Under PM Surya Ghar guidelines, solar modules must meet MNRE technical specifications which typically require:
- **Performance warranty:** 25 years (e.g., 80% output after 25 years)
- **Manufacturing defects warranty:** 10-12 years

**Source:** MNRE technical specifications and Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM)

---

### Q4: I want 4 kW on my house in Kopargaon. How much subsidy do I get, and do I still need net metering from MSEDCL?

**Expected Answer:**  
For a 4 kW system, you will receive **₹78,000 CFA** (same as 3 kW) because CFA is capped at 3 kW capacity. No additional subsidy is provided beyond 3 kW.

**Yes, you need net metering** from MSEDCL (Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company). Net metering is mandatory for all grid-connected rooftop solar installations. MSEDCL will:
1. Inspect and approve your system
2. Install a bidirectional net meter
3. Commission the installation

The net meter tracks electricity you export to the grid and import from the grid, with billing adjusted accordingly.

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(e) — CFA cap; DISCOM net metering requirement for grid-connected systems

---

### Q5: My vendor says I must pay full cost first and subsidy comes later. Is that right, and in how many days is the subsidy released?

**Expected Answer:**  
Yes, under the standard capex mode, you typically pay the vendor first, and CFA is disbursed by the DISCOM/implementation agency in **two installments**:

1. **First installment (90% of CFA):** Released after installation and commissioning of the system
2. **Second installment (10% of CFA):** Released after submission of the performance report

**Timeline:** The exact number of days varies by DISCOM/state, but the process typically takes:
- Commissioning verification: 7-30 days after installation
- First installment: 30-60 days after commissioning
- Performance report: After 6-12 months of operation
- Second installment: 30-45 days after report submission

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(i)

**Note:** Always verify the specific timeline with MSEDCL for Kopargaon area.

### Q7: How much Central Financial Assistance (CFA) will I get if I install a 2.5 kW rooftop solar system on my house?

**Expected Answer:**  
For a 2.5 kW system in general category states, you will receive **₹69,000 CFA**.

**Calculation:**
- First 2 kW: 2.0 × ₹30,000 = ₹60,000
- Additional 0.5 kW: 0.5 × ₹18,000 = ₹9,000
- **Total CFA: ₹69,000**

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(h) — This is the example calculation provided in the official guidelines.

---

### Q8: Are imported solar panels (non-DCR modules) eligible for central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana?

**Expected Answer:**  
**No, imported solar panels (non-DCR modules) are NOT eligible** for Central Financial Assistance under PM Surya Ghar.

**Requirement:** Solar modules must be manufactured domestically using domestically manufactured solar cells. This is the Domestic Content Requirement (DCR).

**Consequence:** Installations using non-DCR (imported) modules are ineligible for CFA under this scheme.

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(m)

**Practical Tip:** Ensure your vendor provides modules from the ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) which lists only DCR-compliant modules.

---

### Q9: If I install a 3 kW inverter with 2 kW of solar panels, is the subsidy calculated on the inverter capacity or the solar module capacity?

**Expected Answer:**  
The subsidy (CFA) is calculated based on the **rated DC capacity of the solar module system**, NOT the inverter capacity.

**Example:** If you install 2 kW of solar panels with a 3 kW inverter, your CFA will be calculated for 2 kW only:
- 2.0 kW × ₹30,000 = **₹60,000 CFA**

**Why this matters:** Some installers might oversize inverters for future expansion or use higher-capacity inverters for efficiency. The subsidy is strictly tied to the solar panel DC capacity.

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(k)

---

### Q10: Can a Group Housing Society (GHS) or Resident Welfare Association (RWA) get rooftop solar subsidy for common facilities and EV charging?

**Expected Answer:**  
**Yes, Group Housing Societies (GHS) and Residential Welfare Associations (RWA) are eligible** for CFA for rooftop solar installations on common facilities, including EV charging infrastructure.

**Key Details:**
- **Capacity limit:** Up to **500 kWp**
- **Calculation basis:** 3 kWp per house in the society/association
- **CFA rate:** Follows the rate specified for additional residential rooftop solar capacity (₹18,000 per kW for general states, ₹19,800 for special category states)
- **Important:** The upper capacity limit includes any individual rooftop plants already installed by residents

**Example:** A housing society with 100 flats can install up to 300 kWp for common areas (3 kWp × 100) and receive CFA at ₹18,000 per kW rate.

**Source:** MNRE Guidelines Section 5(e)
